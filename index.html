THE COMMITMENT

Reset the rhythm together

Nine days is a long time away from our routines. For our students, this week will feel a lot like the first day of school, and that is a gift: every expectation we reteach now becomes the rhythm that carries us through the second quarter. The commitment on our wall still holds. This week, we bring it back to life one routine at a time.

This week’s commitment

Reset it. Inspect it. Recognize it. Before every transition, give students the full picture:

Body: Where are you, and what are you doing?
Voice: What voice level do you use?
Action: What exactly do you do next?

Then start the timer, walk the room with the clipboard, and catch students getting it right. Nothing builds momentum like hearing your name called for doing the right thing!

When a student stalls, reach for CAR: Clarity (Is the next step explicit?), Access (Are the task and materials within reach?), and Regulation (What support helps the student rejoin?). The expectation stays steady; the adult move adjusts.

Before calling Restorative

Our response sequence keeps teaching at the center: clipboard documentation → warning 1 → move seat → set a timer or give extra time → Tap In / Tap Out → call Restorative.

Each warning goes on the clipboard with a calm “That’s one,” and the lesson keeps moving. When a student does leave the room, the clipboard warning and the Restorative form travel together, so the Restorative team can plan a strong return to class.

MORNING MEETING

The 15-Minute Neuro-Regulated Morning Circle

Morning Meeting runs Monday through Thursday, and Friday follows the established Cypher plan. Use the provided slides and lesson content so students encounter the same language and practice across classrooms. Keep the routine consistent while strengthening how students engage with the lesson. Connect each practice to a choice students can use later in class or during a transition.

Regulate · 2Orient · 2Connect · 3Practice · 3Commit · 3Transition · 2

Protect the six phases and their minutes. The welcome is the ready screen, and Relax or Excite stays inside the two-minute Regulate phase.

In the Practice phase, use See It → Name It → Do It: show the expectation, ask students to identify what makes it successful, then have them rehearse it. Use CAR to give each student a clear, accessible, regulated way to participate.

Teacher move

Open the grade-band lesson before students arrive. Review the facilitator notes, prepare one action for students to practice, and close by naming where they will use it that day.

Now in place · Intensive Restorative Interventions · Grades 3–8

Intensive Restorative Interventions give students more focused support to reflect on their actions, understand their impact, and repair relationships. Through guided reflection and conversation, students practice naming what happened, considering who was affected and how, and seeing the situation from another person’s perspective.

Reflect. Repair. Restore. The purpose is to develop empathy, self-awareness, and responsible decision-making while helping students return to learning with a clear next step. Meaningful accountability includes understanding the impact of an action, practicing a different response, and following through on repair.

Teachers help connect this work to the classroom: share observable facts and the impact on learning or relationships, reinforce the student’s agreed next steps, and notice when the student puts those skills into practice. Consistent adult follow-through helps reflection become growth.

The daily rating is most useful when a student hears what happened and what to try next. Give feedback during the class or setting you taught, then enter that rating in Emergent Tree. Each instructor submits the rating for their own class. Mentors use the completed ratings for check-ins and pattern review.

One student, four classes

ELA enters ELA; Math enters Math; Science enters Science; Social Studies enters Social Studies. Ground the score in an observable action and tell the student what it means. For example: “You had your materials ready and started after the first direction. That was Responsible.”

Paper trackers are optional. They can help students see progress, but they do not replace digital entries. If access, class assignments, mentors, or the roster look wrong, flag the issue promptly so feedback follows the student across the day.

CULTURE CORNER

Inspect what we expect

Great routines run on a simple cycle: give the expectation, check it, and celebrate it. Give the direction, start the timer, and walk the room with your exemplar and clipboard. Start with the basics, like names on papers and problem one attempted: “You have two minutes. I’m checking that your name is on your paper and you’ve started number one.” Then shine a light on every student who is on it.

What the walkthrough looks for this week

A Morning Entry or Do Now projected before students enter (middle school, this is our biggest gap); the teacher walking and checking after giving directions; warnings tracked on the clipboard; and Body, Voice, Action given before each transition. If a start stalls, determine whether the next adjustment is in Clarity, Access, or Regulation.
