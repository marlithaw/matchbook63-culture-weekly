Morning Meeting

The 15-Minute Neuro-Regulated Morning Circle

This week’s Morning Meeting uses the same trauma-responsive Tier 1 circle that regulates the body before instruction begins. With four lesson days before Friday’s Cypher, each 15-minute lesson follows the same six-phase arc:

Regulate→Orient→Connect→Practice→Commit→Transition

The repeated structure gives students a predictable way to notice their internal state, use a regulation strategy, connect with others, practice a skill, name a commitment, and transition into learning. 👉 This unit leans hard on the connect phase. Students will be sharing about their families, their food, their languages, and their traditions. Give that share-out its full time. The lesson does its work in the two minutes where a student says something true about home and the room holds it.

🌿 This week’s grounding practice: Feet on the Floor

Before a hard conversation, plant both feet flat. Press down. Name three things you can hear.

Ten seconds. You can do it standing in the doorway before a student walks in.

Regulation is not something we only teach kids. A dysregulated adult cannot de-escalate anyone. Try it before your next redirect and notice what changes in your voice.

Why Friday completion matters

Our Friday Reflect and Repair lunch sessions are for students who did not meet their weekly goal. We can only identify those students when their ratings run through Friday. Right now that is seven students.

Every missing block is a student we cannot invite into repair, and repair is the whole point of the system.

⚠️ This week’s DBRC action

Ratings are still coming in day by day rather than block by block. A student can have Friday data and still be missing several class periods inside that week.

👉 Rate every block, in the moment. Enter it in Emergent Tree the same day. Use the rubric’s observable descriptors, involve students in understanding their ratings, and name the next Safe, Respectful, or Responsible behavior to practice.

DBRC outcomes continue to be reviewed alongside classroom observations, documented Tier 1 interventions, 504 implementation, and other relevant student information. The MTSS Behavior Request remains the single entry point for ongoing behavior support and DBRC consideration.

LiveSchool

Create the opportunity to earn it

LiveSchool is our schoolwide system for student incentives and rewards. Please do not routinely hand out food, candy, drinks, or other incentive items during the day simply because a student completed an assignment or met an expectation. Instead, use the item as something students can work toward through LiveSchool points:

“You can earn LiveSchool points for meeting this expectation.”
“Once you have enough points, you can use them to purchase the reward through the LiveSchool Store.”

If you already have reward items you would like students to earn, coordinate with the student’s homeroom teacher so the reward can be distributed at an appropriate time. The goal is simple: do not just give the reward—create an opportunity for students to earn it.
