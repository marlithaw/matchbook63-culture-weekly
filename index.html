Everyone Belongs

This week is Disability Awareness Week at Matchbook. Our DREAM students and students receiving special education services are our classmates, teammates, and friends, and every word and action from the adults in the building shows students what belonging looks like.

The first week back showed what this team can do. Teachers walked the room with clipboards in hand, practiced new moves in the Coaching Clinic, and welcomed students back into strong routines. This week, that same consistency becomes the way every student is included: a clear next step, a real way to participate, and a respectful word every time.

Here’s to a week where every student feels seen, valued, and part of the team. Let’s ignite it!

THE COMMITMENT

Inspect what we expect, in every room

Week two of the quarter is where routines become habits. The priorities for every classroom are simple and visible: give the expectation, start the timer, walk the laps, and record what you see. When every room runs the same way, every student knows how to belong in it.

Timer

Laps

Clipboard

Keep giving Body, Voice, Action before each transition, then catch students getting it right and say their names out loud. Classroom time is learning time: recess is the free time, and every minute inside the room goes to learning.

The response sequence

Teacher interventions · noted on the teacher’s clipboardRestorative support
  1. 1WarningA calm “That’s one.”
  2. 2Move seatA fresh spot to reset.
  3. 3Timer or extra timeTime to settle and finish.
  4. 4Tap InRestorative support comes to the room. The student stays with the class.
  5. 5Tap OutThe student goes to the Restorative Room, regulates, and returns.

Each intervention is noted on the teacher’s clipboard, and the lesson keeps moving. A well-timed Tap In often prevents a Tap Out, so use it whenever it is workable. When a Tap Out is needed, the interventions already tried are reported on the Restorative form, so the Restorative team knows what has been done and can plan a strong return to class.

Intensive Restorative Intervention (IRI)

When repeated behavior moves beyond classroom-level intervention, IRI gives students focused, adult-guided time to reflect on what happened, understand who was affected, and complete a real repair. Students keep their academic work, accommodations, and services throughout, then return to class with a clear next step.

Choice→Impact→Accountability→Repair→Re-entry
Reflect

Name what happened and who was affected, and see it from another person’s perspective.

Repair

Choose a repair and carry it out. A repair is complete when it is signed off, not just said.

Restore

Return to learning with empathy, self-awareness, and a plan for re-entry.

The teacher’s part Share observable facts and the impact on learning or relationships Reinforce the student’s agreed next steps on return Notice when the student puts the skill into practice

In both lanes, profanity directed at an adult or a Level 3 behavior goes straight to the Deans. The Deans assign every IRI placement, and the family is called that day.

The daily rating is most useful when a student hears what happened and what to try next. Give feedback during the class or setting you taught, then enter that rating in Emergent Tree. Each instructor submits the rating for their own class. Mentors use the completed ratings for check-ins and pattern review.

Ground the score in an observable action and tell the student what it means: “You had your materials ready and started after the first direction. That was Responsible.”

Rate it in the moment. A rating entered during or right after the class is the one that helps the student, and a missing rating is not a zero. Paper trackers are optional. They can help students see progress, but they do not replace digital entries. If access, class assignments, mentors, or the roster look wrong, flag the issue promptly so feedback follows the student across the day.
