Keep students in the room

Every name on our flame is a promise to students, and this week that promise is kept by the adult who owns the room. The priorities stay simple and visible: give the expectation, set the timer, walk the lap, and record what you see. The response sequence

Teacher interventions · noted on the teacher’s clipboardRestorative support
  1. 1WarningA calm “That’s one.”
  2. 2Move seatA fresh spot to reset.
  3. 3Timer or extra timeTime to settle and finish.
  4. 4Tap InRestorative support comes to the room. The student stays with the class.
  5. 5Tap OutThe student goes to the Restorative Room, regulates, and returns.

Each intervention is noted on the teacher’s clipboard, and the lesson keeps moving. Tap In comes first. A well-timed Tap In brings Restorative support into the room and often prevents a Tap Out, so the student keeps the instruction. When a Tap Out is needed, the interventions already tried are reported on the Restorative form, and the visit stays short and purposeful: the student regulates, reflects, and returns. The Restorative team logs the time out and the time back on every visit, so the minutes a student spends away from class are visible and kept small. After a Tap In or a Tap Out, the teacher’s part is the two-minute return: a private welcome, a quick review of the expectation, and a clear restart.

Intensive Restorative Intervention (IRI)

When repeated behavior moves beyond classroom-level intervention, IRI gives students focused, adult-guided time to reflect on what happened, understand who was affected, and complete a real repair. Students keep their academic work, accommodations, and services throughout, then return to class with a clear next step.

Choice→Impact→Accountability→Repair→Re-entry
Reflect

Name what happened and who was affected, and see it from another person’s perspective.

Repair

Choose a repair and carry it out. A repair is complete when it is signed off, not just said.

Restore

Return to learning with empathy, self-awareness, and a plan for re-entry.

The teacher’s part Share observable facts and the impact on learning or relationships Reinforce the student’s agreed next steps on return Notice when the student puts the skill into practice
Lane 1 · Tier 1 students3rd

time sent out in one day, or the 5th in one week, leads to IRI.

Lane 2 · Tier 2 and 3 · DBRC students2nd

time sent out in one day brings a Dean review, so the highest-needs students receive supportive intervention sooner.

In both lanes, profanity directed at an adult or a Level 3 behavior goes straight to the Deans. The Deans assign every IRI placement, and the family receives a ParentSquare message that day. For next-day IRI, have the packet or online work ready by the end of the day; for same-day IRI, a Restorative team member stops by for the work, so students keep learning throughout.

LIVESCHOOL

Recognition students can use

Tier 1 · Our foundation
Points are how students know we noticed

LiveSchool is our primary system for recognizing individual students, which makes awarding points the foundation of our Tier 1 behavior supports. Students invest in a system they can predict. When the same choice earns the same recognition in every class, every day, a point means something. When it depends on the room or the day, students stop expecting it, and then stop working for it. Every other layer of support, from DBRC to IRI, builds on this one.

Consistent→Predictable→Trusted→Invested

The recognition that shapes behavior

Points land hardest when they name one student’s choice, in the moment. A class sweep has its place; the recognition that changes behavior is specific, immediate, and reaches the students who are hardest to catch.

Praise for every correctionGoal: 4 : 1 or better

Say the student’s name, then the expectation: “Responsible: materials out before the timer.” The point follows the words.

Four moves this week
1Name it, then log it

Say the student’s name and the expectation, then award the point in the moment.

2Catch the hardest to catch

Students on a DBRC and students back from IRI first, within the first five minutes of class.

3Four to one

Four specific praises for every correction, every period, before a Tap In.

4Honor the purchase

When a student buys a game or a brain break, make time for it, even if it is not part of your usual routine.

The 4 : 1 goal is the ratio teachers are commonly trained toward; observed middle school classrooms average about 1.7 : 1 (Royer and Ennis, 2024).
