THE COMMITMENT

It started with a shared commitment

Our shared commitment becomes visible in the first five minutes of every start. When students enter in the morning, change classes, or return from lunch or specials, have the First Five slide projected before they enter. Students should be able to answer: Where do I go? What do I need? What do I do? What do I do when I finish? Where is the work?

This week’s commitment

Slide up. Clipboard in hand. Pencil ready. Scan, reinforce, and redirect. For the next two weeks, give immediate, specific recognition when students enter, get materials, begin, and sustain the routine.

When a student has not started, use CAR: Clarity—is the next action explicit? Access—can the student reach the task and materials? Regulation—what support allows the student to rejoin? Keep the expectation and adjust the adult move.

CULTURE CORNER

The First Five

Our classroom focus remains clarity in the first five minutes of instruction. Have the First Five slide ready before morning entry, a new class period, or a return from lunch or specials. It should show where to go, needed materials, the first action, what happens when finished, and where the work lives.

What the walkthrough looks for

Slide visible and readable before entry; assigned seats and materials accessible; students starting without waiting for another explanation; teacher scanning with clipboard and giving specific feedback. If a start stalls, determine whether the next adjustment is in Clarity, Access, or Regulation.

The slide supports the routine; adult presence and reinforcement teach it. Choose one small change to rehearse after feedback, then look for students starting with fewer prompts the next day.

Expectation-practice trailer: Still in progress. Continue using the existing expectation resources and See It → Name It → Do It practice; no new video is assigned this week.

STUDENT CULTURE IN ACTION

When IGrow Up

I build what comes next.

On their visit to The Match High School and Career Center, eighth graders put words to the futures they imagine: cardiologist, engineer, entrepreneur, welder, and more. Then they got to work together—visualizing a bridge, making design choices, testing ideas with their hands, and seeing the different shapes a shared challenge can take.

The most compelling part was the thinking between steps: one student holding a piece steady while another adjusted it; a group looking at its design and deciding what to try next. “When I grow up” became something students could practice in the present—imagining, building, listening, and revising with a team.

Students also explored an individual work booth and saw a demonstration of how its sound-dampening design can create a quieter workspace without disrupting people nearby. It offered another way to picture what a working environment could feel like.
