DBRC Quick Guide

Tier 2 weekly scoring on SAFE, RESPECTFUL, RESPONSIBLE.

Overview

A Weekly Card on Our Three Values

A DBRC scores a student on the three expectations every student at Wendell Phillips already knows: SAFE, RESPECTFUL, RESPONSIBLE. Each is rated on a 0–5 scale. The weekly averages go into the Tier 2 DBRC Dashboard alongside restorative room visits, ISS, and OSS.

Because the card runs on the same three values as the rest of the building, a student on a DBRC is not being measured against a private standard. They are being measured against what we ask of everyone, more closely and with more support.

The weekly goal

DBRC average of 4.0 across the three values — a Total DBRC Points of 12 for the week.

Scoring

The 0–5 Scale

Each value is scored 0 to 5. Three values, so a perfect week totals 15 points and the target is 12.

ValueWhat it coversWeekly target
SAFEBody, hands, and movement keep the student and others safe.4.0
RESPECTFULWords and tone toward adults and peers.4.0
RESPONSIBLEMaterials, work, and following through on expectations.4.0
Weekly Total

How the Weekly Score Is Built

The dashboard calculates two things and then combines them.

  1. Total DBRC Points — the three weekly averages added together. Target is 12.
  2. Total Consequence Deductions — drawn from restorative room visits, ISS, and OSS logged that week.
  3. Weekly Total Points — DBRC points minus deductions. This is the number that drives Tier 2 status.

A student can earn strong value scores and still land low if the week carried consequences. That is intentional: the card measures the whole week, not only the blocks that went well.

Status

Tier 2 Status

The dashboard assigns a status from the weekly total.

StatusWhat it means
On WatchThe student is in Tier 2 and the card is being monitored. Continue and adjust.
Move to Tier 3Tier 2 is not producing movement. The team looks at what is actually driving the behavior.
A blank week reads as Move to Tier 3 in the dashboard. Missing data and a failing card look identical in the numbers — enter every week, even a rough one.
Requesting a DBRC

A DBRC Is Requested, Not Started

A teacher does not start a DBRC directly. It is requested on the MTSS Behavior Request, with Tier 1 supports documented, and the multidisciplinary team decides.

A DBRC is a Tier 2 intervention, which means the team owns it with you. A mentor gets assigned, the family gets contacted before the card exists, and someone tracks the dashboard weekly. A card started informally has none of that and quietly fails within three weeks.

You can also raise a student at a collaborative culture meeting.

Mentors

Every Card Has a Named Mentor

Each student on the dashboard is assigned a mentor who owns the daily relationship and the weekly entry. The mentor is chosen by relationship, not by role — the adult this student will actually talk to.

If no adult on staff fits that description for a particular student, that is itself the finding, and building the relationship comes before the card.

Rating Practice

Two Rules That Matter Most

1 · Every day starts fresh

A hard Monday does not set the ceiling for Tuesday. This is the rule most often broken and the one that decides whether a card works — a student who believes the week is already lost has no reason to try on Wednesday.

2 · Rate on observable information

Rate what you actually saw, not your overall sense of the student. This protects the card in both directions.

Rating too high destroys the data and the student's trust at the same time — they know which days went badly, and a score they did not earn tells them you were not really watching. Rating too low because the week felt hard, or because of something that happened outside the rating window, makes the card feel unwinnable and the student stops trying.

The question is always the same: what did I observe, against this value, in this window?

Reading the Data

What the Numbers Tell Us

PatternWhat we do
At or above 4.0 average, sustainedBegin fading the card.
Approaching 4.0Continue. Adjust one value focus, not all three.
Well below 4.0, sustainedThe card is not the right tool. Escalate and examine what is driving the behavior. We do not simply add consequences.
One value far below the other twoA specific skill gap, not a general behavior problem. Target that value directly.
Strong DBRC points, high deductionsThe student is doing well in structured time and struggling elsewhere. Look at the settings the deductions came from.
Fading

When the Card Ends

The card fades in phases — full weekly scoring, then reduced, then student self-scoring with mentor confirmation, then exit.

The mentor relationship does not end. The card is scaffolding. The relationship is the building.

If a card is not working for a student in your room, bring it to a counselor or the culture team. A card nobody has adjusted in six weeks is usually a card nobody is using.
