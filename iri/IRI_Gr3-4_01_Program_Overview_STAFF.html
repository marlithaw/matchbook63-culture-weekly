Intensive Restorative Intervention

A one-hour, adult-guided reset for 3rd and 4th graders. Students keep their learning, practice one skill, and leave with a plan to repair and return.

WhoGrades 3 and 4, grouped togetherLengthOne hourRatioAbout 5 students per adult
The Skill

One skill, practiced every session

Every IRI hour teaches the same skill so students can carry it back to class. The skill lives in the moment between what happens and what a student does next.

PauseNotice what set me off and what my body is doing.
ChoosePick a response from more than one option.
RepairTake a real step toward the person or place affected.
What IRI is
  • A structured hour with the student's own classwork
  • Instruction in Pause, Choose, Repair
  • Guided repair using the Repair Packet and Repair Menu
  • A planned return to class, with an adult checking in
What IRI is not
  • In-school suspension or a holding room
  • Silent sitting as a consequence
  • A verdict that the student is the problem
  • A placement for talking or minor refusal
The Framing

Impact and identity, not shame

Students this age are increasingly tuned to what peers think of them. IRI does not ask students how others see them. It asks what their choice did to the people around them and who they want to be.

Impact
"What did that choice do to the people around you?"
Identity
"What do you want to be true about you?"
Ownership
What someone else said still mattered. The choice after the reaction is the student's.
Placement

Who is placed, and who decides

The Deans assign every IRI placement. Teachers do not place students directly. Placement rules follow the same two-lane flowchart used for grades 6–8.

Level 3 behavior (physical aggression, fighting, threats, throwing objects at people)Both lanesImmediate
Profanity directed at an adultBoth lanesImmediate
Repeated Restorative Room visits in one dayBoth lanesSame day or next day, per the current flowchart count
Talking, minor refusal, seat-change disputesNot IRITeacher-managed or Restorative Room

IRI is reserved for serious and repeat behavior. Many 3rd and 4th grade Restorative Room entries this fall describe Level 1 talking and refusal after redirects. Those stay with the teacher and the Restorative Room, so the IRI hour keeps its purpose.

The Hour

Six segments, every session

The order never changes, so students always know what comes next. Work runs in short stretches with an adult beside the group. Quiet is used briefly; long silent sitting is not part of the design.

0:00SettleCalm body, feelings check-in
0:05LessonInteractive TV lesson on one skill, with response points
0:17RepairRepair Packet beside an adult. Draw, write, or tell
0:30ResetPlanned movement
0:35AcademicsClasswork; manipulatives for math, or writing tied to the repair
0:50Share & planOne sentence for next time; re-entry plan

Student Voice

Heard first, then accountable

The student perspective note
The facilitator summarizes the student's account of what happened in a short, neutral note to the teacher. The raw packet is not forwarded. The note shares what the student experienced; it is not a finding about the adult.
The adult's part
Before the student returns, the teacher decides whether anything from the adult side is worth naming. A short statement, such as "I could have said that privately," shows students that repair runs both ways.
Re-Entry and Data

Where the skill gets tested

Re-entry
  • The student returns with the plan named in the share-out.
  • The repair step happens the same day or the next morning.
  • A trusted adult holds the morning check-in on the Plan for Tomorrow sheet.
  • A returning student moves up a level and never repeats the same session.
Data
  • The session log feeds the after-school culture coaching clinics and the MTSS student review.
  • Clinic topics come from patterns; attendance is informed by data, not assigned.
  • Patterns are discussed in groups. Individual adults come up only in 1:1 coaching.
