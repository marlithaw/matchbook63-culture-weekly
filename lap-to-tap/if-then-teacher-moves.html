From Lap to Tap · Teacher moves

If this, then that

General teacher moves organized by what the student is doing. Each one starts with the least invasive response. The goal every time is the same: the student back in the learning.

Every behavior has a rootIn the moment, the response is to the impact on learning.
Action. Impact. Correction. Return.Name only what was seen and heard. Correct the action, not the child.
Least invasive firstPrivate before public. The smallest move that restores the learning.
Praise ahead of correctionAbout four positives for every correction, and precise about what was done right.

1Starting the work

a student has not started
give a What to Do direction that names the first physical action: “Pencil on number one.” If the student is still stuck, check privately what the task is asking, or offer a choice of entry: with the teacher or with a partner.
a student asks a question that is real but badly timed
delay and return. Acknowledge it in one line, say when it will be answered, point back to the task, and keep moving: “Good question. After your first point.”
a student says “I don’t get it” or refuses to work
diagnose before assuming defiance. Privately: “What’s getting in the way?” Then reduce the task: a dot on the hard problem, go on to the next one. Return when promised and start at the dots.
a student’s head is down or the student is asleep
check in privately at eye level, in a quiet voice: “Sit up with me. What do you need to get going?” Return with the same calm visit a second and third time, or offer a reset (water, standing to work). Head down on its own is never a Tap Out.
a student finishes early
mark the work, then hand over the next step that is already planned: “Check your work, then read or do extended work.” Seat quick finishers away from students who could become their audience.
several students make the same mistake
pause the class once. Name it as common, not as one student’s error, reteach it, and check for understanding with a response every student gives at once.

2During instruction

students are doing what was asked
notice it first. A silent mark on the paper, precise praise that names the expectation, and positive narration of students who are ready before anyone is corrected.
a student calls out
use a non-verbal hand-up cue and keep teaching. Call on the student once the hand is up, so the question is honored and the routine is retaught without stopping the lesson.
a student is doing something small that distracts (tapping, fidgeting, side comments)
use proximity while teaching. Stand near, tap the page if needed, and keep the lesson going without saying the student’s name.
the room is not ready to listen
give the attention signal, stand still, scan, and wait. Do not talk over students. Thank the students who are ready first.
a student keeps going after the expectation was stated
give one short warning that names the step: “[Name], warning, voices are off.” Said once, in as few words as possible, so there is nothing to argue with.
several students keep going and no single source is clear
correct the group without naming anyone, then follow up individually and privately later: “For anyone continuing, I’m marking.”
a student mutters or tries to get the last word
use planned ignoring. Tally it and keep teaching. The expectation was already said, and answering the last word feeds the audience.
a student argues or talks back
defer the conversation and limit the exchange: “I hear you. Come see me at the end of the block.” Then return to teaching. No back and forth in front of the class.

3Climbing the ladder

the behavior continues after a warning
move to a seat change. Calm and private, to a seat chosen before class: “Bring your paper to Reset 1.” A preset seat leaves nothing to negotiate and changes the audience.
the behavior continues after a seat change
place a written reflection on the desk with no lecture. The student stays in the room and moves from performing to thinking. Any grade band of the reflection can be used, based on the student’s ability.
the behavior continues after the reflection, or the lesson cannot go on
request Tap In early, at Level 2, without naming or spotlighting the student: “I need Tap In support with at least one student.”
support is on the way
disengage and protect the lesson. Tally, keep teaching, and document on the seating chart and lap tracker. Re-engaging would hand the student a stage.
other students start watching or joining in
name the choice for everyone: “If you’re choosing to engage with it, as a spectator or a participant, that is where your focus is.” Then narrate and reward the students who are doing it right.
between any two steps
tally it and keep moving. Once the expectation has been said, the mark is the response.

4The hallway and the return

a conversation has to happen outside the room
stand side by side, away from the door window, with the teacher between the student and the class. Hold the expectation high and leave the label off: “You fell short, but then you bounce back. So you choose.”
more than one student is involved
separate them. Spectators go back in first. The primary student goes back last, so no one walks back into an audience.
a student comes back from Tap In or Tap Out
hold the two-minute re-entry conversation, then give a clean start and a positive contact before the period ends.
documentation is needed
complete it while the Tap In responder holds the class, so the record is made without leaving students unsupervised.
Level 3 behavior is administrative and still goes to the Restorative Room. Safety comes before every move on this page.
